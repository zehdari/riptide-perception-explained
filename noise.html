How far to trust a detection

The detector says where an object is, not how sure it is. Mapping adds that: a covariance per detection, set by range, view direction and how fast Talos was moving.

The short version
  • Every detection gets a covariance (a 3×3 "how far off" matrix), drawn as an ellipsoid.
  • Stereo depth is worse than direction, so the ellipsoid is a cigar along the line of sight, growing with range squared.
  • Turning or moving while the frame is taken fattens it; back-to-back frames count as less than spread-out ones.
  • The numbers are guesses from old thresholds. Nereus scatters about ten times less; measure on the robot.

The detector leaves the covariance fields at zero. The Kalman filter (Chapter 7) needs a measurement covariance \Rm to know how much each detection should move the estimate. It comes from one function, detection_covariance, with parameters under noise: in config.yaml. Every figure here calls the browser's copy of it.

The noise model

A stereo measurement has two error directions: along the camera ray (depth) and across it (bearing, where on the image the object sat). Each gets its own standard deviation. With r the distance from the camera:

\sigma_\perp = b + k_\perp\, r, \qquad \sigma_\parallel = b + k_\parallel\, r + k_2\, r^2
  • b = 1 cm: base error at any range.
  • k_\perp = 0.003 m per m: a 3 mrad bearing error (about five pixels in Nereus), mostly from mask edges.
  • k_\parallel = 0.008 m per m and k_2 = 0.003 m per m²: depth error.

Why depth error grows with range squared

Stereo measures disparity, the pixel shift between left and right images. Depth is z = fB/d (focal length f in px, baseline B), so a fixed matching error \delta d gives

\delta z = \frac{z^2}{f B}\,\delta d

The ZED X Mini has a 50 mm baseline and Nereus renders at f = 1864 px, so fB ≈ 93 px·m: one pixel of disparity error at 2 m is 4.3 cm. k_2 = 0.003 corresponds to quarter-pixel matching. Averaging up to 500 mask points (Chapter 4) shrinks independent pixel noise, but not error the points share.

Motion turns timing error into position error

Mapping looks up the camera pose at the image's timestamp. If stamp and pose disagree by \delta t, turning at \omega swings the detection sideways by \omega\,\delta t\, r, and moving at v shifts it by v\,\delta t. With assumed stamp error t_s = 0.03 s:

\sigma_\perp^2 \mathrel{+}= (\omega\, t_s\, r)^2, \qquad \Rm_\text{cam} \mathrel{+}= (v\, t_s)^2\, I

Rates come from the odometry message nearest the stamp (last 200 kept, motion_at). Turning dominates: 0.5 rad/s adds 4.5 cm sideways at 3 m; 0.3 m/s adds only 9 mm.

Putting it together

With \hat u the unit ray to the detection, the along-ray variance replaces the lateral one in that direction. Then the same TF rotation \Rm_{pc} that moved the detection takes it into the object's frame (map, or torpedo_frame for holes):

\Rm_\text{cam} = \sigma_\perp^2 I + (\sigma_\parallel^2 - \sigma_\perp^2)\,\hat u \hat u^\top + (v t_s)^2 I, \qquad \Rm = \Rm_{pc}\, \Rm_\text{cam}\, \Rm_{pc}^\top

By 4 m, depth error is four times the bearing error. So a view from the side pins a direction a straight-on view can't.

Where the numbers came from

They weren't fit to data. The old mapping published detection spread as covariance, and the torpedo task waited for under 0.0005 m² (2.2 cm σ). The new defaults were set so the filter passes those checks after similar looking time: at 1.5 m, 2.9 cm along the ray and 1.5 cm across. The config says: "measure the real scatter to tune."

Nereus scatters about ten times less than the model: 0.5 cm along the ray at 4 m versus 8.7 cm. That doesn't mean the model is too cautious on the robot. Nereus has no stereo-matching failures, and a 15 s hold misses errors that stay put (a biased centroid, slightly-off calibration). Tune noise.* by running the same hold script on the robot.

Back-to-back detections aren't independent

A Kalman filter assumes each measurement's error is fresh. Frames 100 ms apart share almost all their error. Count ten per second as independent and the filter gets overconfident within a second. So a detection \Delta t after the object's last accepted one is inflated (inflation):

\Rm \leftarrow \Rm \cdot \min\!\Big(\max\!\big(1,\ \tfrac{\tau}{\Delta t}\big),\ 30\Big), \qquad \tau = 0.3\ \text{s}
  • More than 0.3 s apart: counts fully. Closer: counts as \Delta t/\tau of a detection.
  • Cap of 30 covers same-frame repeats (\Delta t is floored at 1 ms).
  • Above 3.3 Hz the information rate is 1/(\tau\sigma^2) per second whatever the frame rate. A faster detector doesn't converge faster; only looking longer or a better view does.

Yaw

Heading has its own scalar filter per object: \psi = \operatorname{atan2}(n_y, n_x) of the detected normal, with a fixed 3° σ (noise.yaw_std_deg) that doesn't grow with range. The same inflation applies. Objects with lock_orientation_to_config (slalom, bin targets) keep the layout's yaw.

What the model leaves out

  • Bias. Covariance is scatter, not offset. A consistent error (mask bleed, depth 2 % long) doesn't average out, but the covariance still shrinks.
  • Correlation between objects seen in the same frame is ignored.
  • Viewing angle. Edge-on is worse than face-on; the model only knows range and motion.
  • Wrong-class detections aren't noise; Chapter 7's gates and the target lists (Chapter 9) handle them.

Read a tight covariance as "seen consistently", not always "seen correctly".
