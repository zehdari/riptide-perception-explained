Mapping · Chapter 9

Frames, targets and the mission tree

Mapping publishes TF frames and covariances. Autonomy drives to the frames, waits on the covariances, and tells mapping which props to look for.

The short version
  • Every prop is a TF frame (like torpedo_frame) plus a pose with covariance.
  • The behaviour tree sets a target (the props to update) and can lock the map while Talos shoots or grabs.
  • RepairCompMpcTree runs the tasks in order; a failed task just moves on to the next.
  • The gate is flown from its prior. The table is fit to its two baskets only if both converge.

This chapter is the interface of the mapping node, mapping.py: its frames, its services, and how the trees in riptide_autonomy use them. It ends with three prop-specific pieces: the bins' classifier, the table fit and the bin fit.

The frame tree

  • odom: where odometry puts Talos. Published identical to world ("until we get SLAM").
  • world → map: set once before a run by the AprilTag calibration. A chameleon_tf node averages the tag board's pose and adopts it (x, y, yaw free; z, roll, pitch locked). map still drifts with odometry.
  • map → offset: published by mapping, a pure translation for where the course sits (Chapter 8).
  • Each map object X gets X_frame under offset; every other object hangs off its parent's frame.

Each object's parent is init_data.<object>.parent. A parent change applies only at the next mapping/reset_mapping. A prior's pose change applies at once: the object jumps to it and forgets its detections.

What gets fused

The detector makes mistakes (a gate leg called a slalom pole, one fire symbol for another), so mapping only fuses the objects autonomy asks for (vision_callback):

  • Targets, via mapping_target: one object or a list like "fire_hole_large, fire_hole_small". The first is the primary. Setting a target resets it (Chapter 7): a well-known object needs 3 agreeing detections to confirm again, anything else 10. Its position is kept.
  • Background targets, via mapping/set_background_targets: always fused, never reset. Their detections move them but don't confirm them.
  • No target: only the closest detected object at least 1 m away is fused.
  • lock_map: stops all fusion. The tree locks from the start until the slalom, between tasks, and while it shoots, drops or grabs.

Detections must also clear confidence_cutoff = 0.2, which never bites on Talos (the detector already cuts at 0.65 forward, 0.8 down). A detection with no TF yet is retried every 125 ms for up to 2 s. mapping/reset_mapping starts over from init_data.

What is published

After every detection message, and at least every 2 s, mapping publishes for each object a PoseWithCovarianceStamped on mapping/<object> and its TF. It also publishes state/mapping (targets, lock) and mapping/fused (objects just fused, like "bin_target1,helmet").

The covariance is the uncertainty of the estimate, including the offset's, so an old sighting grows less certain as Talos travels.

  • Unconfirmed = "not seen". Position variances are raised to at least 1.0 m², angles to 0.1 rad², the old mapping's "no data" values.
  • Yaw only. lock_orientation_to_config keeps the prior yaw; point_yaw_at_parent (table items) faces the table's centre; down-camera objects ignore detected orientation.
  • Output slew is off. output.max_speed and output.max_yaw_rate_deg are 0 on Talos, because the MPC already smooths frame motion.

Tree nodes that talk to mapping

NodeWhat it does
SetMappingTargetCalls mapping_target with target_object and lock_map; fails if the call doesn't return within time_limit_secs.
getCovarianceWaits up to 5 s for the next mapping/<Target> message and outputs its error (below).
MapConvergedSucceeds once every object in a comma list has an error below target_cov. Answers at once, and only trusts messages received after it started ticking.
SeenRecentlySucceeds if the object was detected at or above min_score (0.2) within max_age_secs, from the detector's topic or mapping/fused.

The error is not a distance. It combines the six diagonal variances, angles divided by 2π:

e = \sqrt{\sigma_x^4 + \sigma_y^4 + \sigma_z^4 + \frac{\sigma_\phi^4 + \sigma_\theta^4 + \sigma_\psi^4}{2\pi}}

Its units are m²; for a round estimate e \approx \sqrt3\,\sigma^2. An unconfirmed object scores \sqrt{3 + 3 \cdot 0.01/2\pi} = 1.73. So 0.0005 means about 1.7 cm of σ, 0.0025 about 4 cm, 0.3 about 42 cm.

RepairCompMpcTree, task by task

RepairCompMpcTree.xml is about 4 100 lines, but simple: tasks run in order, each in ForceSuccess, so a timeout or failed check moves on to the next task. Nothing calls mapping/reset_mapping.

Three search subtrees do most of the work:

  • MiniSearchMpc (slalom, magnets): park in front of the prior, set the target, wait 5 s, then poll the covariance once a second until it passes or time runs out.
  • SearchMpc (torpedo): sweep arcs around the estimate. When SeenRecently (last 0.5 s) or MapConverged passes, centre the detection and hold until converged or 2 s out of sight. Widen the sweep after 20 s with nothing.
  • DownwardSearchMpc (baskets, table items, bins): skip if already converged. Else hover over the estimate, follow detections, and after 3 s without one spiral out from 0.25 m to 2 m.

While a prop is in view, the down-camera search re-aims every 0.5 s at the latest detection itself (LatestDetection), and at mapping's frame only when there is none. A new target's frame only moves once four detections agree (Chapter 7), so aiming at the frame alone can hold a prop at the edge of the view until the search gives up.

Things to watch

  • The table check lasts one second. Both baskets must pass 0.0005 in a single 1 s window, never retried. Otherwise no table fit.
  • Pickups 2–4 may not refine. If a background target already brought an item under 0.5, the target isn't set, the map is still locked, and the claw aligns on the older estimate.
  • The bins can block the magnets. If stopping the bins classifier fails, the magnet task's start_binary_classifier fails with "already running" and is skipped. The magnet classifier is never stopped.
  • The slalom assumes the forward camera. It relies on the detector starting on ffc.
  • The gate is flown from the prior. Its searches are disabled (an AlwaysFailure), so a moved course shifts the gate pass, and the return, until another prop is mapped.

Two identical targets: the binary classifier

The bin has two fire vinyls that the down camera can't tell apart. The binary classifier sorts fire detections into bin_target1 and bin_target2 before the estimator sees them (binary_classifier.py).

  1. mapping/start_binary_classifier starts acquire_first: detections fill a rolling buffer (at most 500, each dropped after 8 s).
  2. When a cluster is dense enough, its centroid becomes instance 1 (track_first). Detections within 0.2 m are fused into it and nudge it (α = 0.15); others are rejected.
  3. Autonomy can freeze the buffer during the first target: nothing is added and nothing expires, so detections of the second vinyl survive. Only the buffer freezes: bin_target1 keeps tracking and following its detections.
  4. mapping/start_binary_classifier_second starts acquire_second: look for a buffered cluster at least 0.4 m from instance 1, and reject live detections within 0.3 m of it. A match locks as instance 2 (track_second) at once.
  5. mapping/stop_binary_classifier returns to normal fusion.

A cluster is the detection with the most neighbours within 0.15 m, plus those neighbours: at least 6, the farthest 10 % dropped, rejected if its mean squared spread is over 0.02 m². Distances are horizontal only. A locked instance's estimate is seeded on the centroid, and its detections skip Chapter 7's acquisition check.

The classifier ignores the bin's layout and can't tell which vinyl is which: instance 1 is just the first dense cluster, usually the one Talos is over.

Fitting the table to its baskets

The table's two baskets, helmet and warning, and its small items are children of table_frame. After mapping both baskets, autonomy calls mapping/center_table_on_baskets. It turns and shifts the table so the measured baskets sit at their config offsets:

  • Yaw lines up the basket-to-basket axis; a correction over 30° is skipped.
  • Seen items stay where they were seen; unseen items move with the table.
  • Afterwards the baskets are pinned: new detections and retargeting don't move them.

On the exact-course Nereus run it ran: Moved table by (-0.036, 0.033, -0.000) in map to fit the baskets, yawed table 0.3 deg; kept pill, plug, nut_and_bolt, bandage where they were seen. The older combined table detection (set_table_pair_enabled) is switched off by the tree.

The bin geometry fit

bin_geometry.py is a self-described temporary competition hack. mapping/fit_bin_geometry fits the bin's CAD model to the two classified vinyls: x, y and yaw in closed form (the configured yaw breaks the 180° ambiguity). It refuses if the baseline differs from CAD by over 10 cm or the residual is over 10 cm. Height comes from the vinyls if they agree within 10 cm, else from config. The magnets and drop targets then sit where the CAD puts them, with variance at least 0.0025 m².
