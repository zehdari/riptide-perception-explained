From a mask to a pose

The mask says which pixels are the object; depth says how far each is. Together they give a few hundred 3D points, and a plane through them gives position and facing.

The short version
  • Sample a grid of pixels inside the mask and turn each into a 3D point using depth and the camera's intrinsics (Chapter 1).
  • Drop points whose depth is far from the rest: mask edges often "bleed" onto the wall behind.
  • Fit a plane. Its normal (the direction straight out of the surface) says which way the object faces.
  • The position is the box centre, pushed out to the points' mean depth.

Most errors in where a prop ends up on the map start here. Nearly every RoboSub prop has a flat face that matters, so the detector samples the mask, makes 3D points, filters them and fits a plane (detection.py, filtering in pointcloud.py). The figure below runs each step on a real frame.

Sampling the mask

All masks in the frame are painted into one full-frame image. Inside each box (shrunk by class_detect_shrink, 0 on Talos), the detector keeps every grid_step = 8 px pixel that is masked. A 200 px box has 625 grid pixels, about half to three quarters masked. Above max_sample_points = 500, 500 evenly spaced ones are kept, so a close box costs the same as a far one.

Because the mask image is a union, a box can pick up a neighbour's mask pixels. It never happens in these Nereus frames; if it did, the depth gate would separate them.

Back-projection

Each sampled pixel (u, v) with a valid depth z (not zero, NaN or infinite, which the ZED reports where stereo failed) inverts the pinhole projection:

x = \frac{(u - c_x)\, z}{f_x}, \qquad y = \frac{(v - c_y)\, z}{f_y}, \qquad \bp = (x,\, y,\, z)

f_x, f_y: focal lengths in pixels, (c_x, c_y): the principal point, both from camera_info. Nereus's forward camera: 1864 px and (956, 585).

At 2 m one pixel spans about 1.1 mm, so the 8 px grid puts samples about 9 mm apart.

The depth gate

Masks are a few pixels loose, and stereo blurs depth across edges: pixels just outside a board read somewhere between the board and the wall (flying pixels). Those points tilt the plane, so the first filter removes them by depth alone (_depth_filter):

m = \operatorname{median}(z_i), \qquad s = \max\!\big(1.4826\,\operatorname{median}|z_i - m|,\; 0.05\ \text{m}\big), \qquad \text{keep } |z_i - m| \le 3\,s
  • Median and MAD (median absolute deviation) barely move even if a third of the points are on the wall.
  • 1.4826 scales MAD to a standard deviation, so depth_mad_scale = 3 is a three-sigma gate.
  • The 5 cm floor (depth_min_spread) protects a head-on board: nothing within 15 cm of the median is removed.
  • It is two-sided: stray points in front go too.

Two outlier passes

Survivors then go through two neighbourhood filters (_outlier_indices):

  • Radius pass. Keep a point if more than k = \min(10,\, 0.8\,n) points (itself included) lie within 1 m.
  • Statistical pass. Drop points whose mean distance to their k nearest neighbours is more than one standard deviation above average.

1 m is bigger than most props, so the radius pass rarely removes anything the gate missed. The statistical pass always trims the sparse rim, a median of 13 % of a patch (up to 21 %) in these frames, without tilting the plane.

The plane fit

With at least min_points = 5 left, fit_plane subtracts the centroid and takes the SVD; the direction of least variance is the normal.

\bar\bp = \frac1n \sum_i \bp_i, \qquad \begin{bmatrix} (\bp_1 - \bar\bp)^\top \\ \vdots \\ (\bp_n - \bar\bp)^\top \end{bmatrix} = U \Sigma V^\top, \qquad \bn = V_{:,3}

This minimises squared point-to-plane distance. The normal is flipped to face the camera (n_z < 0).

The published orientation has its +z axis along the normal; x and y follow a fixed rule, so there is no singularity. Mapping uses only the normal's heading, \operatorname{atan2}(n_y, n_x), as the object's yaw (Chapter 7). That is what lets autonomy line up square to the torpedo board.

Where the object is

The position is the bounding-box centre, back-projected at the points' mean depth:

\mathbf c = \Big(\tfrac{(u_b - c_x)\, \bar z}{f_x},\; \tfrac{(v_b - c_y)\, \bar z}{f_y},\; \bar z\Big), \qquad (u_b, v_b) = \text{box centre}, \qquad \bar z = \bar\bp_z

The box centre stays stable when filtering removes points unevenly. It is not quite where the centre ray meets the plane (ray_plane_intersection, used for the torpedo board); on the 37° board in the figure the two differ by under half a centimetre. The gap grows for lopsided masks, such as objects cut off by the image edge.

What comes out

Each box with five or more points left becomes one Detection3D: class, confidence, centroid and quaternion in the camera's optical frame, with no covariance (Chapter 6 decides its weight). A box whose points don't survive publishes nothing. That is the common failure: a mask mostly on invalid depth (image edge, reflective surface, open water) is seen but never mapped.

The filtered samples also feed the debug point cloud and the dots on the annotated image, so RViz and Foxglove show exactly what the plane was fitted to.
