Appendix A

The math in one place

Every equation the detector and mapping run, in the order a detection meets them, with Talos's parameters. Nothing here is new: each block links back to the chapter that explains it.

Notation

Camera optical frame: z forward along the lens axis, x right, y down. The map frame is the course frame: z up, the water surface at z = 0. Odometry positions are in map too, so "where the course sits in odometry" is an offset between the prior layout and what the cameras find.

A mask becomes a pose

From Chapter 2.

Grid-sample the mask every 8 px (at most 500 samples) and back-project each through its depth z:

x = \frac{(u - c_x)\, z}{f_x}, \qquad y = \frac{(v - c_y)\, z}{f_y}, \qquad \bp = (x,\, y,\, z)

Keep the points whose depth is within three robust standard deviations of the median (the depth gate):

m = \operatorname{median}(z_i), \qquad s = \max\!\big(1.4826\,\operatorname{median}|z_i - m|,\; 0.05\ \text{m}\big), \qquad \text{keep } |z_i - m| \le 3\,s

then a radius pass (more than \min(10, 0.8n) neighbours within 1 m) and a statistical pass (mean distance to the k nearest neighbours below the mean plus one standard deviation). The plane is the direction of least spread of what is left:

\bar\bp = \frac1n \sum_i \bp_i, \qquad \begin{bmatrix} (\bp_1 - \bar\bp)^\top \\ \vdots \\ (\bp_n - \bar\bp)^\top \end{bmatrix} = U \Sigma V^\top, \qquad \bn = V_{:,3}, \quad \bn \leftarrow -\bn \text{ if } n_z > 0

The published position is the box centre (u_b, v_b) back-projected at the points' mean depth, and the orientation puts body +z on the normal:

\mathbf c = \Big(\tfrac{(u_b - c_x)\, \bar z}{f_x},\; \tfrac{(v_b - c_y)\, \bar z}{f_y},\; \bar z\Big), \qquad R = \big[\, \mathbf e_1 \;\; \mathbf e_2 \;\; \bn \,\big], \quad \mathbf e_1 = \frac{\bn \times \mathbf a}{\lVert \bn \times \mathbf a \rVert},\ \mathbf e_2 = \bn \times \mathbf e_1

\mathbf a is the camera x axis, or y when the normal is within 26° of x; the first column is negated if \det R < 0.

The torpedo board

From Chapter 3.

One plane is fitted to the pooled points of every symbol on the board (fire and blood required, ambulance and fire engine optional), anchored at the mean symbol centroid \mathbf a. Each symbol and circle centre is then intersected with that plane along its camera ray:

\mathbf d = \frac{K^{-1}(u, v, 1)^\top}{\lVert K^{-1}(u, v, 1)^\top \rVert}, \qquad t = \frac{\bn \cdot \mathbf a}{\bn \cdot \mathbf d}, \qquad \mathbf p = t\,\mathbf d

With an in-plane basis (\mathbf e_u, \mathbf e_v), the board centre is the middle of the projected points' extent, and the four holes split two and two along the fire-to-blood axis:

\mathbf c = \mathbf p_0 + \tfrac12\big(\min_j u_j + \max_j u_j\big)\,\mathbf e_u + \tfrac12\big(\min_j v_j + \max_j v_j\big)\,\mathbf e_v, \qquad s_h = (\mathbf p_h - \mathbf p_\text{fire}) \cdot \frac{\mathbf p_\text{blood} - \mathbf p_\text{fire}}{\lVert \mathbf p_\text{blood} - \mathbf p_\text{fire} \rVert}

The two smallest s_h are fire's holes; of each pair, the one nearer its symbol is the large hole.

How much a detection is worth

From Chapter 4.

Standard deviation across the camera ray and along it, at range r, with the vehicle turning at \omega and moving at v and a stamp error t_s:

\sigma_\perp^2 = (b + k_\perp r)^2 + (\omega\, t_s\, r)^2, \qquad \sigma_\parallel = b + k_\parallel\, r + k_2\, r^2
\Rm_\text{cam} = \sigma_\perp^2 I + (\sigma_\parallel^2 - \sigma_\perp^2)\,\hat u \hat u^\top + (v t_s)^2 I, \qquad \Rm = \Rm_{pc}\, \Rm_\text{cam}\, \Rm_{pc}^\top

where \Rm_{pc} rotates the camera frame into the object's parent frame. A detection \Delta t after the object's last accepted one shares most of its error, so

\Rm \leftarrow \Rm \cdot \min\!\Big(\max\!\big(1,\ \tfrac{\tau}{\Delta t}\big),\ 30\Big), \qquad \Delta t \ge 1\ \text{ms}

Detected yaw is the heading of the normal in the parent frame, \operatorname{atan2}(n_y, n_x), with a fixed 3° standard deviation, inflated the same way.

Tracking one object

From Chapter 5.

A three-state Kalman update in the object's parent frame, in Joseph form:

\boldsymbol\nu = \bz - \hat\bx, \quad \mathbf S = \P + \Rm, \quad \K = \P\mathbf S^{-1}, \qquad \hat\bx \leftarrow \hat\bx + \K\boldsymbol\nu, \qquad \P \leftarrow (I - \K)\,\P\,(I - \K)^\top + \K\Rm\K^\top

Once the track has 10 accepted detections, a detection is rejected when

\boldsymbol\nu^\top \mathbf S^{-1} \boldsymbol\nu \;>\; 16.27 \;=\; \chi^2_3(0.999)

and 30 rejections in a row restart the track. Yaw is a scalar filter on the wrapped innovation \operatorname{wrap}(\psi_z - \hat\psi), gated at \chi^2_1(0.999) = 10.83. A map object's track ages between sightings by the odometry drift since it was last seen, \P \leftarrow \P + \Q(\Delta s, \Delta t) (below).

Acquisition. Until a track has accepted a detection, detections are held for 4 s. Two agree when

\lVert \bz_a - \bz_b \rVert \;\le\; \max\!\Big(0.3\ \text{m},\ 3\sqrt{\lambda_\text{max}(\Rm_a + \Rm_b)}\Big)

The largest group of detections that agree with one member starts the track once it has 4 members. It needs 12, spanning at least 2 s, when its mean \bar\bz is far from where the map expects the object:

(\bar\bz - \hat\bp)^\top \Big(\boldsymbol\Sigma_{\hat\bp} + \tfrac1m \textstyle\sum_{j} \Rm_j\Big)^{-1} (\bar\bz - \hat\bp) \;>\; 16.27

The course map

From Chapter 6.

Every map-parented object is the prior layout, turned by the course yaw, plus where the course sits in odometry, plus that object's own layout error:

\bp_i = \Rm(\psi)\,\bp_i^{\,0} + \bo + \bd_i

The offset random-walks with distance travelled and time:

\Q(\Delta s, \Delta t) = \operatorname{diag}\big(q_{s}\Delta s + q_{t}\Delta t,\ \ q_{s}\Delta s + q_{t}\Delta t,\ \ q_{s,z}\Delta s + q_{t,z}\Delta t\big)

A track with 10 detections becomes a fix (\bz_k, \P_k) at the path length s_k it was last seen. Two fixes of one object less than 1 m of travel apart merge:

\P = \left(\P_a^{-1} + \P_b^{-1}\right)^{-1}, \qquad \bz = \P\left(\P_a^{-1}\bz_a + \P_b^{-1}\bz_b\right)

With the fixes sorted by path length, one offset node per fix, one deviation per object seen, and the course yaw, the solve minimizes

J = \frac{\psi^2}{\sigma_\psi^2} + \sum_k \big\|\bz_k - \Rm(\psi)\bp^{\,0}_{i(k)} - \bo_k - \bd_{i(k)}\big\|^2_{\P_k^{-1}} + \|\bo_1\|^2_{(\boldsymbol\Sigma_o + \Q_1)^{-1}} + \sum_{k} \|\bo_{k+1} - \bo_k\|^2_{\Q_{k}^{-1}} + \sum_i \rho\!\left(\|\bd_i\|^2_{\boldsymbol\Sigma_L^{-1}}\right)

\Q_k is the drift between consecutive fixes (plus 10^{-6} on the diagonal), \Q_1 the drift up to the first, \boldsymbol\Sigma_o the offset's start prior and \boldsymbol\Sigma_L the layout prior.

The layout term is Cauchy-robust, solved by iteratively reweighted least squares (the rotation linearized about the current \psi each pass):

\rho(r^2) = s^2 \log\!\left(1 + \frac{r^2}{s^2}\right), \qquad w(r^2) = \frac{1}{1 + r^2/s^2}, \qquad s = 2

A new set of fixes is solved from zero, from every start that blames one prior (its weight set to 0 for the first pass), and from the last answer; the lowest J wins. An object with w < 0.5 is released. Every object's estimate, seen or not, is then

\hat\bp_i = \Rm(\hat\psi)\,\bp_i^{\,0} + \hat\bo + \hat\bd_i, \qquad \boldsymbol\Sigma_i = \mathbf J\,\mathbf A^{-1}_{[\psi,\bo,\bd_i]}\,\mathbf J^\top + \Q(\Delta s, \Delta t)\;\big[\,+\,\boldsymbol\Sigma_L \text{ if unseen}\big]

\mathbf A is the normal matrix at the solution, \mathbf J = [\,\partial \Rm(\psi)\bp^{\,0}_i/\partial\psi \;\; I \;\; I\,], and \Delta s, \Delta t the travel since the newest fix.

What is published

From Chapter 7.

Until an object is confirmed by fresh detections while targeted (10, or 3 that agree with a known estimate), its published covariance is floored:

\boldsymbol\Sigma_\text{pub} = \boldsymbol\Sigma + \operatorname{diag}\big(\max(1 - \Sigma_{jj},\ 0)\big), \qquad \sigma^2_{\text{roll}} = \sigma^2_{\text{pitch}} = 0.1, \qquad \sigma^2_\text{yaw} \ge 0.1
