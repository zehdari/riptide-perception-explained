Cameras and calibration

To turn a pixel into a position, the software needs the exact numbers for how the camera makes images. Underwater, the factory numbers are wrong.

The short version
  • A camera is described by its intrinsics (focal length and image centre, in pixels) and its lens distortion.
  • The flat window in front of each ZED X Mini bends light, so the in-air factory calibration is wrong underwater.
  • The team calibrates underwater with a ChArUco board and OpenCV, and hands the result to the ZED SDK.
  • Stereo depth error grows with the square of distance.

From a point to a pixel

The vision code treats each camera as a pinhole: every ray passes through one point, the optical centre. The camera frame sits there, with z out of the lens, x right and y down (the optical frame, e.g. talos/ffc_left_camera_optical_frame). A point (x, y, z) lands on pixel

u = f_x\,\frac{x}{z} + c_x, \qquad v = f_y\,\frac{y}{z} + c_y \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \propto \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} f_x & 0 & c_x \\ 0 & f_y & c_y \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}}_{K} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix}
  • f_x, f_y: focal length in pixels.
  • (c_x, c_y): the principal point, where the lens axis hits the image.
  • K: the same four numbers as a matrix.
  • \propto: “proportional to”, equal up to a scale factor. K(x, y, z)^\top comes out as (z u,\ z v,\ z); dividing by the last entry, z, gives (u, v, 1). So every point along one ray lands on the same pixel: a pixel alone gives a direction, not a distance, which is why the detector needs depth.

Talos's forward camera (underwater, 1920×1200): f_x = 1701.5, f_y = 1702.4, c_x = 935.2, c_y = 579.4, a 59° wide view.

A pixel alone gives only a direction, ((u - c_x)/f_x,\ (v - c_y)/f_y,\ 1). The depth image supplies the distance (Chapter 4).

Lenses bend straight lines

Real lenses curve straight edges near the image corners. OpenCV's plumb bob model (Brown–Conrady) uses five numbers (k_1, k_2, p_1, p_2, k_3). With x' = x/z, y' = y/z and r^2 = x'^2 + y'^2, the lens moves them to

x'' = x'\,(1 + k_1 r^2 + k_2 r^4 + k_3 r^6) + 2p_1 x'y' + p_2(r^2 + 2x'^2), \qquad y'' = y'\,(1 + k_1 r^2 + k_2 r^4 + k_3 r^6) + p_1(r^2 + 2y'^2) + 2p_2 x'y'

then u = f_x x'' + c_x, v = f_y y'' + c_y.

  • Radial (k): positive is pincushion, negative is barrel.
  • Tangential (p): a lens not quite parallel to the sensor.

The ZED SDK rectifies (undistorts) every image before publishing, so image_rect_color is already a pinhole image.

Why water breaks the factory calibration

ZEDs are calibrated in air at the factory. Talos's cameras look through a flat window, and light bends crossing from water into air (Snell's law):

n_\text{air} \sin\theta_a = n_\text{water} \sin\theta_w, \qquad n_\text{water} \approx 1.33
  • The view narrows by about 1.33, and the focal length in pixels grows by the same factor. Hence f_x \approx 1700.
  • Pincushion distortion appears, seen as k_1 \approx +0.27 in both of Talos's calibrations.
  • The stereo geometry changes, so factory depth is off by roughly the refraction factor.

So the team calibrates underwater and sets self_calib: false. Otherwise the SDK would "correct" the stereo geometry at start-up with in-air assumptions.

Two lenses make depth

A ZED X Mini is two cameras about 50 mm apart. A point shows up shifted sideways between them by the disparity d = u_\text{left} - u_\text{right}:

z = \frac{f\,B}{d}, \qquad \delta z \approx \frac{z^2}{f\,B}\,\delta d

B is the baseline (lens spacing). With f = 1701.5 px and B = 49.75 mm:

  • 2 m away: 42 px disparity; a quarter-pixel matching error is 1.2 cm of depth.
  • 6 m away: 14 px; the same error is 11 cm.

That z^2 is why mapping trusts depth less than direction (Chapter 6).

The SDK matches pixels on the GPU. That is a 1D search only if the images are rectified so a point sits on the same row in both. That needs the stereo extrinsics (rotation R and translation T, left lens to right). Forward camera: T = (-49.75,\ 0.02,\ 0.98) mm, and R turns the right lens about 1.35° about vertical.

Why a ChArUco board

Calibration photographs a target of known geometry from many angles and solves for the numbers that best explain it.

  • Chessboard: sub-pixel corners, but the whole board must be visible.
  • ArUco markers: each identifies itself, but corners are less precise.
  • ChArUco: a chessboard with a marker in each white square. Any visible part gives precise, identified corners.

Underwater the board is often tilted, cut off or in murk, so this matters.

Calibrating, step by step

  1. Board in the water: rigid, flat, well lit.
  2. Capture stereo pairs with picture_taker.py (launched by zed.launch.py). It reads the raw side-by-side stereo_raw/image_raw_color; each capture_image call saves PNGs to cal_images/left/ and cal_images/right/.
  3. Cover everything: dozens of pairs, near and far, tilted, reaching the image corners.
  4. Solve with OpenCV: detect ChArUco corners (aruco), calibrateCamera per lens (K, distortion), then stereoCalibrate (R, T). The forward calibration fixes tangential terms at zero and frees k_3; the 2026 downward one does the opposite.
  5. Check: RMS reprojection error well under a pixel, baseline near 50 mm, R near identity. Drop any image with a much larger error and re-solve.
  6. Install: save an OpenCV YAML (Size, K_LEFT, D_LEFT, K_RIGHT, D_RIGHT, R, T in mm) in riptide_hardware/cfg/. Point optional_opencv_calibration_file at it in ffc_config.yaml or dfc_config.yaml, keep self_calib: false, restart the ZED node. The detector reads K from rgb/camera_info.

The solver script and board dimensions live on the robot (/home/ros/zed_cals). Current files: tirpak_ffc_charuco_3.yaml (forward), zedxm_dfc_2026_cal.yaml (downward).

Where the cameras are on the robot

Mapping also needs each camera's place on Talos (base_link → optical frame). That is not calibrated: it comes from CAD and measured tilts in talos.yaml, published by the URDF as a fixed TF.

  • Forward: about 11 cm ahead, 12 cm to starboard, 10 cm up, pitched 0.05 rad (2.9°) down.
  • Downward: level fore and aft, 12 cm to starboard, pointing straight down.

A 1° pitch error puts a prop 3.5 cm off at 2 m. Always go through TF rather than swapping axes by hand.

How a bad calibration shows up

  • Flat props come out tilted or curved, so plane normals point off.
  • Distances off by a constant factor: wrong focal length or baseline.
  • Curved edges in image_rect_color.
  • Depth holes on textured surfaces: the matcher searches the wrong row.

If orientations look slightly off, first check the camera uses the right calibration file.
