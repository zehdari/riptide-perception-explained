Mapping · Chapter 10

One mission in Nereus

Every earlier chapter in one run: Talos's whole software stack swimming the RoboSub 2026 course in the simulator, on a course that is not where its map says.

The short version
  • The full stack ran the competition tree in Nereus twice: on the mapped course, and with the course moved 72 cm (the torpedo another 1.27 m).
  • On the exact course every prop is confirmed, the table is fit and every task runs.
  • On the moved course the slalom fixed the map, but the torpedo was blamed on odometry drift, pulling unseen props back off the truth.
  • So a table basket was never found and the table never fit. Both runs still finished: tasks fail soft.

The run

Nereus simulates the pool, props, Talos's dynamics and sensors, and renders both ZED cameras' colour and depth. Everything else is the robot's own software, unchanged: YOLO and tensor_detector, mapping, the EKF, the MPC and RepairCompMpcTree. Nothing in the stack knows where the simulator put the props.

The map is init_data, the RoboSub 2026 layout as the team measured it. Two runs:

  • Exact course. Every prop exactly where the map says.
  • Moved course. Every prop shifted 0.6 m east and 0.4 m south (72 cm) (the offset mapping estimates), and the torpedo another 0.9 m east and 0.9 m north (a 1.27 m layout deviation).

Both ended SUCCESS, but every task fails soft (Chapter 9), so the moved-course failures only show in the data. The false slalom pole on a gate leg (Chapter 3) is harmless: the map is locked through the gate.

What went wrong 1: the torpedo moves the whole map

The slalom put the map in the right place. Then the badly placed torpedo was explained mostly as drift, dragging every unseen prop back off the truth.

  • Slalom (65 s). Four detections agree, ten confirm it. One sighting can't tell a moved course from one moved prop, so the solve splits the 72 cm by the uncertainties; almost all goes to the offset (94 % with the configured priors; see the end of this chapter): (0.57, −0.38) m against the true (0.60, −0.40) m. Unseen props are now 68 cm closer to the truth.
  • Torpedo. 1.27 m off after the offset. The prediction is wide by then: 23 m of travel (about 21 cm of drift per axis at drift_var_per_m_xy = 0.002) plus the 2° course-yaw prior (63 cm at 18 m from the origin). So four detections suffice, and the solve now has two props that disagree.
  • The solve keeps the torpedo. Drift and a small course turn are cheaper than a 5σ layout error. The newest offset node jumps to (1.13, −0.12) m. The torpedo ends 11 cm off, but the gate, bins and table end 62–77 cm off, no better than their priors.

In Nereus odometry drifted under 15 cm, so drift was the wrong explanation here; on the robot it may be right. The balance is set by drift_var_per_m_xy, course_yaw_std_deg and layout_std_xy, best tuned from a robot run with measured drift.

What went wrong 2: a basket that was never found

The map was off, so the down camera searched the wrong spot for the warning basket. It caught the basket two or three frames at a time; mapping needs four that agree. No estimate, no table fit.

  • DownwardSearchMpc hovers 1.5 m over the basket's estimate, follows it in view, else spirals, for up to 60 s. The helmet basket was found at 3:39 (error 0.00023, about 1.2 cm σ).
  • The warning basket's estimate hung off the offset the torpedo had pulled the wrong way: 69 cm from the truth.
  • Every 14 s the spiral swept the real basket across the edge of the view (warning in view: following the detection). But "following" re-aims at warning_frame, which had not moved.
  • A reset target needs 4 detections within 0.3 m in the last 4 s (Chapter 7). Bursts of 2–3 aged out, the covariance stayed at 1.73, and the search timed out.

The tree logged helmet,warning, not both converged below 0.0005: the table keeps its prior. On the exact course: Moved table by (-0.036, 0.033, -0.000) in map to fit the baskets, yawed table 0.3 deg.

No single rule is a bug, but together they deadlock when the estimate starts more than about half a camera footprint off. Any one fix would have worked here: steer by the raw detection until acquired, keep held detections longer than 4 s, or keep the torpedo from moving the offset (Chapter 8).

Every prop against the truth

On the moved course every prior starts 72 cm off (the torpedo 1.58 m). On the exact course errors stay at a few centimetres: the slalom ends 6 cm off, the torpedo 12 cm.

What mapping said

Mapping logs only when something changes. Moved-course lines, with the tree's target changes; tap one to move the replay there.

What the simulator does and doesn't show

  • Depth is clean. Detections scatter about ten times less than the noise model assumes (Chapter 6). Real stereo depth is noisier.
  • Odometry is good. The EKF drifted under 15 cm over the 133 m run. On the robot the offset must absorb real drift.
  • The start-time drift is huge. In simulation the node reads the clock before the first /clock message, so its first predict adds about 1.8 × 109 s of drift to the offset's prior (17 900 m² at the start). On the robot it starts at the configured 1 m. The runs here are barely affected, but it is worth fixing in mapping.py: set last_predict_time on the first odometry or detection, not in the constructor.
  • Everything else is real: models, thresholds, filters, the tree's checks and timing.
