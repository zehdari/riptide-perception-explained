Mapping · Chapter 7

Tracking one object

Each mapping object has its own small filter. It starts at the layout's guess, waits for a few detections to agree, then averages in the ones that fit and refuses the rest.

The short version
  • Each prop has its own Kalman filter: a best guess of where it is plus a covariance for how sure it is.
  • The first detections are held back until 4 agree within 4 s, so one stray detection can't move anything.
  • After 10 detections, any detection more than about 4σ off is rejected. 30 rejections in a row and mapping starts over.
  • Until a prop is freshly seen while targeted, mapping publishes a huge covariance, so the behavior tree waits.

This chapter follows one object's track, the estimate built from its own detections. For a child object (a torpedo hole in torpedo_frame, a table item in table_frame) the track is the estimate. For a course prop in map, the track becomes a fix that the course map solves with (Chapter 8). The code is MapEstimator.observe.

Mapping only fuses classes in its target list (set by autonomy via the mapping_target service) plus any background targets. With no target set, it fuses only the closest object more than 1 m away. Detections under 0.2 confidence are dropped first.

The update

Objects don't move, so there is no motion model. The state is a position \hat\bx with covariance \P. A detection \bz measures it directly, with covariance \Rm from Chapter 6:

\boldsymbol\nu = \bz - \hat\bx, \quad \mathbf S = \P + \Rm, \quad \K = \P\mathbf S^{-1}, \qquad \hat\bx \leftarrow \hat\bx + \K\boldsymbol\nu, \qquad \P \leftarrow (I - \K)\,\P\,(I - \K)^\top + \K\Rm\K^\top
  • The gain \K (how far one detection moves the estimate) is near I when the track knows little, near zero when it knows a lot. It works per direction.
  • The Joseph form of the covariance update stays symmetric and positive definite under rounding (_update_position).
  • Odometry drifts, so before each new sighting a course prop's covariance grows by 0.002 m² per metre travelled horizontally.

Yaw

Heading uses the same update in 1D, with the innovation wrapped to (-\pi, \pi] (179° and −179° are 2° apart). Prior 30°, each detection 3°. Its own gate is \nu^2/S > 10.83 (χ² 1-DOF, 99.9 %); a rejected yaw is skipped while the position still counts.

Gating

The Mahalanobis distance is how many standard deviations a detection missed by, measured along the direction of the miss. Its square d^2 = \boldsymbol\nu^\top \mathbf S^{-1} \boldsymbol\nu follows a χ² distribution (a known "how often by chance" curve) with 3 degrees of freedom, and exceeds 16.27 only 0.1 % of the time:

\boldsymbol\nu^\top (\P + \Rm)^{-1} \boldsymbol\nu \;>\; 16.27 \;=\; \chi^2_3(0.999) \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{reject}
  • The gate turns on after 10 accepted detections (gate_min_updates). Before that, the agreement rule protects the track.
  • It stops lookalikes of the same class and bad depth readings. A rejected detection changes nothing.
  • 30 rejections in a row (gate_max_rejects, 3 s at 10 Hz) means the track locked onto the wrong thing or the prop moved. Mapping drops the track and reacquires from the prior.

In Nereus: after the claw carried off the pill, mapping logged pill: 30 rejections in a row (last 36.7 sigma), reacquiring, and half a second later pill 4 detections agree started a new track where the pill really was.

Acquisition by agreement

A fresh track moves most of the way to whatever it sees first, so one false detection could drag it off. So detections are held back until enough agree (_agree):

  • With no accepted detection yet, detections are held for up to 4 s (acquire_window_s).
  • Two agree if within \max(0.3\text{ m},\ 3\sqrt{\lambda_\text{max}(\Rm_a + \Rm_b)}) of each other.
  • The biggest group of mutually close detections wins. At 4 members (acquire_detections) they are fused in time order and the rest dropped.
  • A group whose mean is d^2 > 16.27 from the map's estimate is far and needs 12 members over at least 2 s. This only bites once another prop has pinned the offset (Chapter 8).
  • Bin detections named by the binary classifier (Chapter 9) skip the hold; they arrive pre-clustered.

It can stall. A prop seen only two or three frames at a time, more than 4 s apart, never reaches four agreeing detections. That is why the searches aim at the raw detection until mapping has acquired it (Chapter 9).

Targeted, established, confirmed

Behavior trees wait for a published variance (say of mapping/torpedo) to fall under a threshold before acting. Three flags per track decide what gets published:

  • Targeted. Only detections while the object is in the target list can confirm it. Background targets improve but never confirm.
  • Established. 10 accepted detections while targeted since the last full reset (confirm_detections).
  • Confirmed. Established, and not asked to re-check since.

Setting a target calls reset(target, verify=True), which clears confirmed. Then:

  • Never established: full reset to the prior covariance, agreement again, 10 detections to confirm.
  • Established before: a verify. It keeps its estimate (widened by drift) and 3 in-gate detections (verify_detections) confirm it. A detection outside the gate means it moved ("moved 62 cm since it was last seen, finding it again") and starts a fresh search.

What gets published

Until confirmed, mapping floors the published position variances at 1.0 m² and angle variances at 0.1 rad² (publish_pose), above every tree threshold. That matches the old mapping, so under threshold still means freshly seen while targeted.

What this doesn't do

  • One track is one object. Two same-class props in view (the bins' targets) would average together, which is why bins use the binary classifier.
  • Agreement picks the biggest cluster, not the right one. A busier lookalike wins until 30 rejections reacquire.
  • Between agreement (4) and the gate (10), nothing filters the target class. Turn on the lookalike right after "4 detections agree" in the demo to see it.
  • A child object's track is only aged when retargeted; its parent carries most of the drift.
