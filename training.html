Vision · Chapter 2

Training the detector

The network that finds props knows nothing about RoboSub until it sees thousands of labelled pictures. How those pictures are made decides most of what it gets right and wrong.

The short version
  • Talos runs a YOLO segmentation model per camera: for each object, a class, confidence, box and pixel mask.
  • Data: real prop videos labelled with SAM, plus auto-labelled synthetic images from Nereus.
  • Images are augmented (rotated, zoomed, mirrored, pasted onto unrelated photos), per class where needed.
  • The trained .pt becomes a TensorRT engine on the robot, named in yolo_orientation.yaml.

What the network learns

The detector runs instance segmentation: it outlines every pixel of each prop. The next step (Chapter 4) samples depth only inside that outline; a box would include the pool wall behind.

The models are Ultralytics YOLO26n-seg ("nano"), small enough for real time on the Jetson Orin. Training fine-tunes (adapts a pre-trained model) yolo26n-seg.pt, already trained on COCO's 80 everyday classes. That is why a few thousand images are enough.

  • Forward model: eleven classes (gate signs, slalom pole, torpedo symbols and openings, bin magnets).
  • Downward model: eight (bin vinyls, the table's items and baskets).

Class lists are in yolo_orientation.yaml; a dataset's class order must match exactly.

Recording the props

The team films each prop with the robot's own cameras (ZED .svo files), so lens, colour and water match: near and far, all angles, clear and murky, robot moving. One minute is a thousand frames. svo_processor converts SVO to rectified video.

Variety beats quantity: a hundred frames from all around a prop teach more than a thousand from one spot.

Labelling masks with SAM

SAM 3 (Meta's Segment Anything Model): click or describe an object in one frame, and SAM masks it through the video. A person fixes drift and assigns classes, following the team's labelling guide.

Labels use the YOLO segmentation format: one text file per image, one line per object: class number, then outline points as fractions of image width and height.

# labels/train/torpedo_004908.txt: class, then x y pairs (fractions of the image)
4 0.5099 0.1583 0.5073 0.1725 0.5047 0.1817 0.5036 0.1825 ...
0 0.6915 0.3958 0.6889 0.4016 ...

Synthetic images from Nereus

Nereus labels for free: it knows which object every pixel belongs to. nereus-dataset generate:

  • places the camera with a sampler: approach (range, bearing, elevation, some roll and pitch) or overhead (altitude, for the downward camera);
  • randomizes water and light across four environments (nominal, clear, murky, overcast);
  • renders the image and an object map, converts it to YOLO polygons, and rejects views where a prop is too far, small or fragmented.

The 2026 forward set has 10 250 images (gate 2300, slalom 1500, torpedo 2000, octagon 1200, bins 2000, plus 1250 backgrounds with no prop); the downward set 7350. Backgrounds teach that an empty wall is not a prop. Synthetic data complements real video; it doesn't replace it.

Augmenting: more variety from the same images

Augmentation changes training images (and their labels) to fake views the model never saw. The team does most of it offline, producing the *_dataset_Augmented sets:

  • Rotation: the robot rolls.
  • Zoom and crop: nearer, farther, partly out of frame.
  • Mirroring: free extra views (careful with props whose sides differ).
  • Copy-paste: cut props out along their masks and paste them onto unrelated photos (e.g. COCO). This is the main defence against false detections, like the gate leg called a slalom pole in Chapter 3.

Some are per class: slalom poles are never rotated, since in the pool they are always vertical.

Training

One Ultralytics command, pointed at a data.yaml listing image folders and class names in order:

yolo segment train model=yolo26n-seg.pt data=data.yaml imgsz=640 epochs=80 close_mosaic=10

An epoch is one pass over every training image. Ultralytics re-augments each image every pass. The deployed models used its defaults:

SettingValueWhat it does
imgsz640images are resized (letterboxed) to 640 × 640, the size the robot runs at
epochs, close_mosaic80, 1080 passes; the last 10 without mosaic, so the model finishes on whole images
mosaic1.0every image is part of a 2 × 2 collage of four random training images
scale, translate0.5, 0.1random zoom ±50 % and shift ±10 %
fliplr, flipud0.5, 0half the images mirrored left-right; never upside down
hsv_h, hsv_s, hsv_v0.015, 0.7, 0.4random hue, saturation and brightness: different water and light
degrees, copy_paste0, 0off: rotation and copy-paste are done offline, where they can be per class

Runs are tracked in Weights & Biases. The forward model on the robot, rs26_ffc_finals, trained for 80 epochs.

Reading the results

About 80 % of images are for training, 20 % held back for validation (scoring only). Split by recording, not frame: neighbouring frames are near-duplicates and inflate the score.

  • Precision: share of detections that were real. Low means false detections.
  • Recall: share of real props found. Low means misses.
  • mAP50: mean over classes of the area under the precision–recall curve, counting a mask right at ≥ 50 % overlap. mAP50-95 averages 50–95 % overlap, rewarding precise outlines.

rs26_ffc_finals: mask precision 0.952, recall 0.968, mAP50 0.948, mAP50-95 0.880. High scores are necessary, not sufficient. If validation improves while the robot gets worse, the model is overfitting (memorising training pictures); add varied data and backgrounds, and test on unseen recordings.

Putting a model on Talos

  1. Copy the .pt into tensor_detector/weights/.
  2. In config/yolo_orientation.yaml, set ffc_model or dfc_model, and the camera's class_id_map in the model's order. Classes are named from this map; unlisted ids are dropped.
  3. Leave export: true: on first start the node builds a TensorRT .engine next to the .pt, falling back to the .pt if export fails. Rebuild after a GPU or TensorRT upgrade.
  4. Check ffc_threshold and ffc_iou against the new confidences, then watch ~/annotated/compressed in Nereus, then the pool.
